The Future of Spatial Computing

David Chen
Oct 28, 2023 12 min read

Spatial computing is no longer a concept confined to science fiction. As hardware matures and software ecosystems expand, we are standing on the precipice of a fundamental shift in how we interact with digital information. It is the transition from screens to environments.

The core premise is seamless integration. Instead of forcing human attention into a bounded rectangular frame, spatial computing projects digital affordances into the physical spaces where human life naturally occurs. This requires a leap not just in optics and processing power, but in UX paradigms.

Designing for Depth

When the Z-axis becomes available, interface design changes completely. UI elements must respect occlusion, lighting, and physical boundaries. Traditional wireframing falls short.

"The best interface is no interface. In spatial computing, the environment itself becomes the canvas, and your physical intuition is the primary input device."

Consider the logic of handling an object in 3D space. It requires physics simulation, affordance cues (like shadows and highlights), and haptic feedback. Here is a basic example of how developers define a spatial entity:

const spatialEntity = new RealityKit.Entity({ mesh: Geometry.Sphere(radius: 0.5), material: new PBRMaterial({ roughness: 0.2, metallic: 0.8, color: '#FFFFFF' }), components: [PhysicsBody, Interactable] }); scene.add(spatialEntity);

As we build these new worlds, the emphasis must remain on human-centric design. Technology should adapt to our physiology, not the other way around. The brands that win the next decade will be those that master invisible, ambient, and deeply contextual experiences.
